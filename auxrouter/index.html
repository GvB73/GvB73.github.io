What you need

  • An audio interface with 4 or more outputs
  • macOS 15.6 or later

First-time setup

  1. Select your interface as the sound output: System Settings → Sound → Output. Or hold Option and click the volume icon in the menu bar.
  2. Click the AuxRouter icon in the menu bar and choose your interface under Interface.
  3. Switch Routing on.
  4. macOS asks for permission to record system audio. Click Allow. AuxRouter uses this only to route the audio; nothing is recorded or stored.
  5. Play something. Outputs 1 and 2 carry left and right; the aux outputs carry whatever you set there.

The aux outputs

SettingWhat it does
NameClick the name to change it, for example "Sub" or "Kitchen"
SourceOff, L only, R only, or mono sum (L+R)
OutputWhich output of your interface it goes to (any output except the stereo pair)
VolumeFrom −∞ (off) to +6 dB
Delay0 to 50 ms, to time-align a sub or a distant speaker
Invert polarityFlips the phase 180°; useful when sub and speakers cancel each other out
IdentifyPlays 1 second of soft noise on this output, so you can hear which speaker it is. Works even when the source is Off.
S (solo)Hear only this channel. Also available for L and R. Several solos can be on at once.

Tips

  • Subwoofer with one input: source Mono sum. The mono sum is 6 dB lower to prevent clipping; raise the volume if needed.
  • Subwoofer with L and R inputs: Aux A on L only to Out 3, Aux B on R only to Out 4.
  • If A and B use the same output, they are added together.
  • AuxRouter has no low-pass filter for the sub; set that on the sub itself.
  • The small level meters show the incoming L/R signal and what each aux output sends.

Interfaces with their own mixer software

Your audio can be routed in up to four places. AuxRouter only adds to what is already there: every channel passes through unchanged, and the aux signals are mixed in on top.

LayerWhat it decides
1. The app (Spotify, a DAW)Which interface and which channels it plays to
2. Audio MIDI Setup → Configure SpeakersWhich channel pair macOS uses for stereo. AuxRouter follows this pair.
3. Your interface's own software (Focusrite Control, RME TotalMix, UA Console…)Which playback channel goes to which physical output
4. AuxRouterAdds the aux signals to the playback channels you choose

Recommended setup

  • In your interface's software, send each playback channel to the output with the same number (Playback 3 → Output 3), and don't also send 1/2 to the aux outputs.
  • Leave Audio MIDI Setup on the standard stereo pair unless you have a reason not to.
  • In AuxRouter, "Out 3" means playback channel 3 as macOS sees it. The name after it, such as "Line 3", comes from your interface.
  • Using a DAW on the same interface? AuxRouter passes its channels through unchanged but adds a little latency. Switch Routing off while recording.

Sound doubled, hollow or in the wrong place?

The same signal is probably routed in two layers. Switch AuxRouter off: if the extra speaker still plays, your interface software is also sending to it. Remove one of the two. The Identify button helps you find which speaker is connected to which output.

Troubleshooting

ProblemSolution
My interface is not in the listOnly interfaces with 4 or more outputs are shown. Connect it and click Refresh devices.
Orange message about the system outputSelect your interface as the sound output in System Settings → Sound.
No sound on the aux outputsCheck the source (not Off), the volume (not −∞) and the permission in System Settings → Privacy & Security.
Everything is silentCheck whether Mute is on. Quitting AuxRouter always gives you the normal macOS sound back.
Sub sounds thin or hollowTry Invert polarity and adjust the delay.

Uninstalling

Quit AuxRouter from its menu and drag the app to the Trash. No drivers or other files are left behind.
